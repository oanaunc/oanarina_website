Your data on your devices

Pace Up is a walking journal and activity recorder. There is no Pace Up account, advertising or analytics. The app saves activity dates, GPS routes, distance, pace, elevation, splits, steps and any available heart-rate summary in private storage on your phone or watch. It also keeps your goals, settings, Waymark locations and text, capsule dates and any photos or voice memos you add. The developer does not receive this local data.

Location, motion and media

Pace Up asks for precise location when you start recording or leave a Waymark. During an active recording, location measures your route and detects returns to Waymarks. Recording can continue when the screen is off or you switch apps, using a user-started foreground service with a recording notification. Finish the workout to stop it. Outside recording, the phone may request a short GPS fix for a Waymark you choose to create or to show GPS readiness before a workout. The phone can use the step sensor with your permission. The watch uses its orientation sensor for the Waymark compass and, with permission on supported watches, reads heart rate during a workout.

When you add a photo, Pace Up keeps a local copy of the image you select or capture through the system. When you record a voice memo, microphone access lasts for that recording. If you choose watch dictation, your system speech provider handles it. Its own settings and privacy policy govern any speech processing it performs.

Health Connect

On Android phones, optional Health Connect permissions let Pace Up read steps, distance and active calories for Today, and heart rate for workout summaries. Pace Up can write a completed exercise session you record, including the activity type, start and end times and any workout note you enter. Health Connect permissions can be reviewed or revoked in Android Settings. Pace Up uses this data for the features you select. It does not sell health data or use it for ads or profiling.

Optional street maps

Street maps are off until you enable them. They use MapLibre to request map tiles, styles and labels from OpenFreeMap over HTTPS. The request can reveal the area displayed on the map and the device IP address. Pace Up draws your GPS trace and Waymark markers locally and does not send those records, their text, or health data to the map provider. OpenFreeMap describes how it handles request and security logs in its privacy policy. Turn off Street maps in Pace Up Settings to use the offline route view.

Optional Wear OS sync

If you enable sync on both devices, Pace Up exchanges workout routes and summaries, Waymark locations and text, and visit events between the paired Android phone and watch. A workout summary can include heart rate if the watch recorded it. This feature uses the encrypted Wear OS Data Layer. Google Play services may relay encrypted traffic over its network. The Pace Up developer does not receive these transfers. Photo and voice-memo files stay on the phone. Turn off sync on either device to stop future transfers. Turning it off on the watch also clears the phone journal copies cached there.

Exports, retention and deletion

On the phone, you can export activity backups and GPX routes to a destination you choose, or open directions for a Waymark in another map app. The destination or map app handles the file or coordinates you send to it. The phone's Data & Privacy screen can delete local Pace Up data. Optional route cleanup removes older detailed GPS traces and keeps activity totals and approximate Terra coverage. Watch Settings can clear the local journal and unsynced workouts. Uninstalling the app removes its local data. If you already saved a copy to Health Connect, a paired device or an export destination, delete that copy separately.

Keepsakes purchases and albums

Pace Up is free to download. Keepsakes is an optional one-time purchase through Google Play, which processes the payment and provides the purchase status to Pace Up so it can unlock or restore the feature. The developer does not receive your payment card details. Albums, their dedications, and the moments you select stay on your phone. If you export a PDF, the destination you choose handles that copy.
